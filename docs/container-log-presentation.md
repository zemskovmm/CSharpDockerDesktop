# Container Logs presentation

The selected container detail has keyboard-accessible Overview, Inspect and Logs tabs. Opening Logs is the only presentation action that opens its Application-owned `IContainerLogs` session. It requests the Application's configured finite recent tail (300 lines by default) and live follow by default. The “Live follow on next open” checkbox selects finite-only or following output on Reopen Logs; changing this checkbox alone does not reopen the stream. There is no Pause control or decision about ingestion-versus-presentation Pause behavior.

The view displays original entry text, including timestamp prefixes, Unicode, CR/LF and partial/continued segments. Metadata separately identifies available original timestamps, unavailable timestamps, stdout/stderr or combined output. Long lines remain horizontally scrollable rather than being rewritten or truncated. A finite-height ListView with ItemsStackPanel recycles its rows. Text selection is deliberate local interaction. No log or daemon text enters automatic logging, diagnostics or reporting.

## Retained search and reading

Plain-text case-insensitive search and stream filters scan the Application's currently retained output, not just the visible page. TTY sessions disable separate stream selection and preserve combined output. Display uses one window of at most 128 source entries; the status explains that window explicitly. Sparse matching windows contain fewer rows. Older matches and Newer matches turn autoscroll off and locate earlier/later matching windows through bounded page reads. They do not request older daemon history. With autoscroll enabled, a search starts at retained history and advances progressively to its latest matching window. With autoscroll disabled, a new query/filter starts at the earliest retained match. Matching rows outside the window remain discoverable through the navigation controls. Counts describe the displayed window, not a total count of all retained matches.

Each dispatcher turn reads/project one bounded scan page, in addition to rereading the current window. Notifications coalesce into at most one queued callback per view lifetime. Scans retain a cursor and fixed pass budget, not a growing collection of pages/results. Producer notifications/revisions do not cancel or restart a pass; each matching page can become visible before the pass finishes. Search/filter changes replace the cursor/query for the next turn without queuing a worker per keystroke. Live updates normally scan forward from the latest displayed window instead of repeatedly scanning all retention. Snapshot/page session and revision guards reject raced reads; identity/lifetime guards reject results before delayed selection/navigation notifications drain. Rejected pages are reread. These bounds are implementation tuning and do not establish workstation latency or CPU targets.

Application metadata never contains log text; bounded pages are deliberate caller content. The presenter retains one 128-entry row collection. A dispatcher turn has only its current-window and scan-page locals; pages are not captured in dispatch callbacks. The control's bounded observable collection refers to the same entry strings, without formatting/copying a complete transcript. Storage is additionally bounded by the Application's text/entry budgets. Mutable partial entries are reread and replaced even when EntryId is unchanged; row metadata and content bindings update together.

Autoscroll off keeps its source-window identity while ingestion proceeds, and updates/recycles rows rather than resetting ItemsSource on every notification. The UI preserves a visible row identity on replacement when possible. Ordinary live output does not jump to the newest window. When retention or Clear removes the reading position, the view moves to the next retained output and displays explicit position-loss text. Evicted rows cannot be reconstructed from old pages. Clear immediately invalidates queued presentation work, empties rows and invokes the Application's local publication barrier. It sends no daemon mutation and does not reopen historical output.

## State and ownership

Opening, live/finite streaming, recovery, completion, cancellation, admission failure and classified terminal failure have text states. Driver unavailability specifically explains that the logging driver cannot supply readable logs. Access denial, not-found, opening deadline, stream capacity, malformed output, daemon HTTP rejection and transport loss are distinct. The presenter uses fixed classified descriptions; original daemon content is only visible after activating its sensitive-content button. Changing failure evidence or reopening hides it again. Retention counters expose evicted entries and discarded characters. Recovery exposes uncertain continuity or matched bounded overlap evidence and always avoids promising gap-free history.

Leaving Logs for Overview/Inspect, closing details, selecting another identity, resource disappearance, changing connection generation, navigation or page lifetime ends presentation ownership immediately. Close detaches signals, invalidates queued callbacks, clears retained strings and synchronously invokes Application close while it still owns that exact SessionId. Its captured asynchronous join is awaited without blocking the UI; page departure also retains/awaits that join. Completion of an old join performs no later close and cannot close a subsequently opened session. Application handles Docker requests, decoding, retention, recovery and producer disposal; Presentation contains none of those responsibilities. Accepted lifecycle operations remain independently session-owned.
